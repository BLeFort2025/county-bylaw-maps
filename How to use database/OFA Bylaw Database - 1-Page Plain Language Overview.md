# 🏛️ Ontario Municipal Bylaw Database & Intelligence System
## Plain-Language 1-Page Overview for OFA Leaders, County Federations & Farmers

---

### 1. What Is This Tool & Why Does It Matter?
Ontario has **444 municipal governments** (townships, towns, cities, and regional counties). Every single month, local town councils enact or update municipal bylaws that directly impact everyday farm operations—from adding tens of thousands of dollars in fees on new barns, to restricting working guardian dogs, regulating fence lines, or imposing stormwater runoff charges on farm properties.

When these local rules change, farmers risk unfair costs and regulatory red tape unless an **explicit agricultural exemption** is written into the law. 

This tool is the **Ontario Federation of Agriculture's (OFA)** province-wide digital map and early warning system. It translates thousands of pages of municipal legal documents into a simple, interactive map so OFA directors, staff, and local farm leaders know exactly where farmers are protected—and where action is needed.

---

### 2. The 7 Key Bylaw Categories Tracked Across All 444 Municipalities

| Bylaw Category | What It Means in Plain English |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Development Charges (DC)** | Building fees charged on new construction. Tracks whether farm buildings (barns, silos, packhouses) and on-farm worker housing are exempt from municipal growth fees. |
| **2. Stormwater Fees** | Runoff user fees based on roof or hard-surface area. Tracks whether agricultural properties and greenhouses are exempt from urban runoff charges. |
| **3. Site Alteration & Fill** | Rules on moving dirt, grading land, and accepting clean fill. Ensures normal farming operations and field drainage improvements are protected from municipal permits. |
| **4. Livestock Guardian Dogs** | Animal control and kennel rules. Ensures guardian and herding dogs are recognized as essential working farm animals—exempting them from pet limits, barking bans, and license fees. |
| **5. Tree / Forest Conservation** | Tree-cutting bylaws. Tracks whether farmers maintain the right to clear fence lines, remove hazard trees, and manage woodlots for normal farming without special permits. |
| **6. Backyard Chickens** | Rules allowing non-farmers to keep small hen flocks in towns. Tracks flock limits (usually 3–6 hens), rooster bans, and biosecurity/welfare standards. |
| **7. Fence Bylaws** | Rules on livestock fencing, electric fences, and shared boundary lines. Ensures local rules allow agricultural fencing and respect the provincial *Line Fences Act*. |

### 3. The Interactive Map: A Simple "Traffic Light" Visual
You do not need any technical training or software installed to use the system. When you open the website link in any browser (on a computer, tablet, or phone), you choose a topic and the entire province lights up:

* 🟢 **GREEN (Protected):** Farm Exemption in Place — Local farmers are protected from fees or restrictions.
* 🔴 **RED (Action Needed):** No Farm Exemption — Agriculture is charged or restricted (a high-priority target for OFA advocacy).
* ⚪ **GREY (N/A):** No bylaw currently exists in that municipality for this topic.
* 🟠 **ORANGE BORDER:** The bylaw is expiring within 18 months and will soon be up for council review.
* 🔴 **RED-ORANGE BORDER:** Our early warning scanner recently caught council discussions about this topic in public minutes or agendas!

---

### 4. Automated Early Warning & 1-Click Advocacy Action

1. **Weekly Council Agenda Scanner:**
   Every Sunday night, the system automatically checks municipal websites across Ontario, reading new council agendas and meeting minutes. If a township quietly starts discussing a bylaw review that could affect farmers, the town flags on our map *before* the vote happens.
2. **One-Click Personalized Letters to Municipal Clerks:**
   If you click on any municipality on the map that lacks a farm exemption (a "Red" municipality), you can instantly generate a personalized, pre-formatted **Microsoft Word advocacy letter**. The system automatically pulls the local Municipal Clerk's email address from our 7,000+ contact directory, fills in OFA's official policy positions, and can even create an email draft in Outlook ready to send.
